Capital exists, but it does not always reach productive economic activity in the appropriate structure.

Products come first

Financial products are designed in advance, so businesses are asked to fit the product rather than the other way round.

Capital is fragmented

Capital, institutions and specialist capability sit in separate places and rarely arrive together.

Structures don’t fit the activity

Terms, timing and repayment are often set without reference to how the business actually earns.

The Financial Engine

Six steps, one loop: from economic need to productive activity, and back into reinvestment.

Reinvestment returns to need

A real business or project need is identified and understood: its cash flows, contracts and constraints.

Illustrative exampleA regional food manufacturer wins a large export order. It needs working capital to buy inputs now, but the buyer pays in 120 days.

A structure is designed around the need, with terms, timing and repayment matched to how the activity earns.

Illustrative exampleThe facility is sized to the order and repaid from the buyer’s payment, rather than through a standard term loan.

The structure is matched to the platform, group company or authorised partner able to deliver it.

Illustrative exampleDelivery sits with the entity that holds the relevant capability and, where needed, the regulatory permission.

Capital whose mandate fits the structure, risk and tenor is connected through the appropriate authorised vehicle.

Illustrative exampleCapital providers looking for short-tenor, trade-linked exposure are matched to the facility.

Capital reaches the underlying activity and is put to productive use.

Illustrative exampleThe manufacturer buys its inputs, fulfils the order and keeps its workforce and suppliers busy.

Performance is tracked, returns flow back, and what was learned informs the next structure.

Illustrative exampleThe buyer pays, the facility is repaid, and the engagement informs how the next order is structured.

Better World

What “Better World” means in practice

Most financial organisations treat impact as a filter, applied after capital has already been allocated. Our approach starts earlier. At BWCI, the outcomes we intend to support shape how capital is structured, owned and deployed.

“Impact should influence the architecture of capital itself.”

That means designing investment structures, ownership and capital flows around long-term productive outcomes, not adding them as an afterthought.

01

Productive enterprise

Capital that reaches SMEs and entrepreneurs, supporting the businesses that create employment.

02

Resilient trade and supply chains

Finance structured around how goods and services actually move, so working capital doesn’t become a bottleneck.

03

Sustainable infrastructure and regenerative business

Patient, long-term capital for productive assets and businesses that restore rather than deplete.

04

Stronger local economies

Capital that circulates in the communities where it is earned, supporting local resilience and, where relevant, financial inclusion.

We only report outcomes we can evidence. Our Impact & Outcomes methodology sets out what we intend to measure and how.In development
