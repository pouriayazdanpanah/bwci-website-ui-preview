Our approach

Our approach: impact built into the architecture of capital

Impact is often assessed after capital has already been allocated. Our approach considers it earlier: when capital is structured, owned, and deployed.

01

Structured

When we design a financing structure, we set its form, terms, and time horizon. At this stage, we consider the intended outcome so the structure fits the activity it supports.

02

Owned

Ownership decides who holds an interest in an activity and who is accountable for it. We consider impact here by setting ownership and governance arrangements that keep the intended outcomes in view while the capital is committed.

03

Deployed

Deployment is where capital reaches productive economic activity: a business trading, or a project being built. We measure impact here by providing capital in a form the activity can use and recording what happens to inform the next decision.

Financial Engine

How this connects to the Financial Engine

The Financial Engine outlines how BWCI responds to an economic need in six steps, from Need to Reinvestment. The approach above runs through it: capital is structured at Architecture, owned through Institution, and deployed through Capital and Activity. At Reinvestment, what we learn informs the next need.

Reinvestment returns to need

  1. A real business or project need is identified and understood: its cash flows, contracts and constraints.

    ExampleA regional food manufacturer wins a large export order. It needs working capital to buy inputs now, but the buyer pays in 120 days.

  2. A structure is designed around the need, with terms, timing and repayment matched to how the activity earns.

    ExampleThe facility is sized to the order and repaid from the buyer’s payment, rather than through a standard term loan.

  3. The structure is matched to the platform, group company or authorised partner able to deliver it.

    ExampleDelivery sits with the entity that holds the relevant capability and, where needed, the regulatory permission.

  4. Capital whose mandate fits the structure, risk and tenor is connected through the appropriate authorised vehicle.

    ExampleCapital providers looking for short-tenor, trade-linked exposure are matched to the facility.

  5. Capital reaches the underlying activity and is put to productive use.

    ExampleThe manufacturer buys its inputs, fulfils the order and keeps its workforce and suppliers busy.

  6. Performance is tracked, returns flow back, and what was learned informs the next structure.

    ExampleThe buyer pays, the facility is repaid, and the engagement informs how the next order is structured.
